# WS-04 — Governance & Factions · Phase A kickoff

```
Workstream WS-04 — Governance & Factions for Orchestra (repo AbscissaOrdinate/Orchestra).

Read first: docs/conops.md (§3, §5 P-8, §6 governance rows), docs/research/m1-findings.md §2, docs/specs/WS-02-architecture.md and WS-03-economy.md (if approved), docs/specs/README.md.

Objective: produce docs/specs/WS-04-governance.md (GOV- requirements) and a Python prototype plan for milestone M2(c). This is the showcase system.

Scope (MVP):
- Government type as a parameter set: selectorate size S, winning coalition W, veto players, succession rule, legitimacy source, ethics/ideology axes, central bank independence (consumed by WS-03), and how political strength is weighted by wealth, status and votes (the V3 precedent)
- Factions as interest vectors over the policy space, with power weights (clout) and approval; faction membership drawn from WS-03 quantiles and sectors; split membership allowed
- Policy space: define the axes and how every budget line, tax, and law maps onto faction utility
- Selectorate mechanics as a game mechanic (not an empirical claim; see m1-findings §2.2): the trade-off between public and private goods, coalition loyalty, challenger risk
- Stability and legitimacy, and their consequences: unrest, coups, elections, revolution (choose a minimal set)
- Reform: changing government structure is a coalition-gated project with a risk of crisis; let players compose a custom government from parameters, not scripts
- Lobbies: faction coalitions on foreign policy, as the link to WS-06
- Ruling classes and ethics: how they constrain the available policies
- Macro-tier reduced form for macro polities

Out of scope: characters and dynasties, detailed elections and parties beyond what the selectorate mechanics need, freeform scripted events.

Decisions to make:
1. The policy space dimensions (keep them few; every one must map onto a mechanic)
2. The faction set: fixed archetypes plus emergent ones, or fully data-driven
3. The stability function and crisis triggers
4. How reform works (stages, costs, failure modes)
5. The interface contract with WS-03 (both directions)

Working method: options → trade-offs → recommendation; at most 5 questions; tag values [V]/[R]/[D]. Use comparative-government examples freely.
Deliverables: spec on a branch with a PR; prototype plan (prototypes/governance/) with scenario runs (e.g., a fiscal squeeze under an oligarchy vs. a democracy); POAM updated.
Done when: every GOV- requirement is testable, a player can build a custom government from parameters, and the WS-03 and WS-06 interfaces are explicit.
```
